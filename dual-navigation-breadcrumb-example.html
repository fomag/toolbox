Social Insurance Number (SIN)

A Social Insurance Number (SIN) is a nine-digit number that you need to work in Canada. The breadcrumb trail above shows this page is part of the "Setting up a new employee" process flow. Use the "Steps in this process" navigation on the right to move through the complete workflow.

Why breadcrumbs matter: The breadcrumb trail indicates this is a sequential process page. The right sidebar shows the full process flow with your current step highlighted, helping you understand where you are in the journey and what comes next.

Why you need a SIN

You need a SIN to:

  • Work in Canada
  • Open a bank account
  • Apply for loans or credit cards
  • Apply for government benefits
  • Report income and file taxes

How to get a SIN

If you are a Canadian citizen, permanent resident, or refugee, you can apply for a SIN:

  • Complete the Application for a Social Insurance Number form
  • Provide proof of identity and residency in Canada
  • Submit your application in person at Service Canada or by mail

Employer Responsibilities

As an employer, you must request a SIN from each employee before they start work. This is a required step in the employee onboarding process.

Tax-Free Savings Account (TFSA)

A TFSA is a registered account that lets you set aside money tax-free throughout your lifetime. The breadcrumb trail above shows this is a topic page in the tax information cluster, not a sequential process. Use the "Related topics" navigation on the right to explore connected information at your own pace.

What is a TFSA?

A TFSA is a flexible savings account available to Canadian residents aged 18 and over. Unlike RRSPs, funds in a TFSA are not tax-deductible, but growth and withdrawals are tax-free.

Key benefits

  • Tax-free growth on your investments
  • Tax-free withdrawals at any time
  • No income restrictions
  • Flexible contribution room
  • Funds remain in your control

Contribution Limits

Your annual contribution room accumulates each year. If you don't contribute the full amount in a given year, the unused room carries forward to future years, giving you flexibility in when and how much you contribute.

Using Your TFSA

You can use your TFSA to invest in a variety of eligible investments including stocks, bonds, mutual funds, and GICs. The key advantage is that all income earned and growth within the account is tax-free.
