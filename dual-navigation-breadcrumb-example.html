Social Insurance Number (SIN)

A Social Insurance Number (SIN) is a nine-digit number that you need to work in Canada or to receive benefits from the Government of Canada. This page is part of the "Setting up a new employee" process workflow, but it's also a topic that relates to many other tax and benefits information.

Pattern combination: This page demonstrates how you can include both a process flow (subway) and related topics navigation in the same right sidebar. The visually distinct sections help users understand the difference—the blue section is a structured workflow, the orange section is exploratory topic browsing.

Why you need a SIN

You need a SIN to:

  • Work in Canada
  • Open a bank account
  • Apply for a loan or credit card
  • Apply for government benefits
  • Report income and file income taxes

How to get a SIN

If you are a Canadian citizen, permanent resident, or refugee, you can apply for a SIN. To apply, you will need to:

  • Complete the Application for a Social Insurance Number form
  • Provide proof of identity and residency in Canada
  • Submit your application in person at a Service Canada office or by mail

Types of SINs

There are different types of SINs depending on your status in Canada:

  • Regular SIN: For Canadian citizens and permanent residents
  • Temporary SIN: For foreign workers and international students

Employer Responsibilities

As an employer, you must request a SIN from each employee. This information is essential for payroll processing and tax reporting.

Requesting from Employees

You must request a SIN from each employee before or when they start working. Once collected, you must keep it confidential and store it securely.

Tax-Free Savings Account (TFSA)

A TFSA is a registered account that lets you set aside money tax-free throughout your lifetime. Unlike RRSPs, funds in a TFSA are not tax-deductible, but all growth and withdrawals are tax-free. This is a standalone topic page with no sequential process—explore related information at your own pace.

Pattern flexibility: This page shows what the sidebar looks like when there's no process flow (no subway). Only the orange "Related topics" section appears, keeping the interface clean and focused. This demonstrates the flexibility of the dual-pattern approach.

What is a TFSA?

A TFSA is a flexible savings account available to Canadian residents aged 18 and over. The key advantage is that all income earned and growth within the account is tax-free.

Key benefits

  • Tax-free growth on your investments
  • Tax-free withdrawals at any time
  • No income restrictions
  • Flexible contribution room
  • Funds remain in your control

Contribution Limits

Your annual contribution room accumulates each year. If you don't contribute the full amount in a given year, the unused room carries forward to future years, giving you flexibility in when and how much you contribute.

Current year contribution limit

The contribution limit changes based on inflation and is indexed annually. Check the CRA website for the current year's limit.

Using Your TFSA

You can use your TFSA to invest in a variety of eligible investments including stocks, bonds, mutual funds, and GICs. The key advantage is that all income earned and growth within the account is tax-free.

Investment options

Most financial institutions offer TFSA accounts with a range of investment options. Talk to your financial institution about which investments are best for your situation.
